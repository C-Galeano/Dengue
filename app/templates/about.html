What does it predict?

For a febrile patient, the probability that the illness is each of three diagnoses:

  • Dengue and Chikungunya: laboratory-confirmed cases.
  • Other febrile illness: suspected arbovirus cases that the laboratory ruled out.
  • Inputs ({{ info.features|length }}): age, sex, days since onset, 11 symptoms, 3 clinical/laboratory findings (petechiae, tourniquet test, leukopenia), 7 comorbidities and the epidemiological context (state of residence and month).
  • Decision rule: the most likely diagnosis, except that dengue is suggested whenever P(dengue) ≥ {{ threshold_pct }}%. That threshold catches at least {{ (info.target_dengue_recall * 100)|int }}% of dengue cases: missing dengue is the most dangerous error.
  • Explainability: every result shows the SHAP contribution of each finding.

Limitations

  • Decision support, not diagnosis. Arboviruses share most symptoms; confirmation requires laboratory tests (NS1, RT-PCR, serology).
  • Zika is not modeled: its public notification form has no clinical fields, so there is nothing to learn from.
  • "Other febrile illness" comes from chikungunya suspects ruled out by the laboratory, because Brazil's public dengue files do not include discarded cases.
  • No numeric lab values: public surveillance data record leukopenia and the tourniquet test as yes/no, not as platelet or white-cell counts.
  • Trained in Brazil. On Colombian data (Tuluá) its dengue signal is close to chance, partly because the Colombian form lacks most of the model's findings. It must be retrained with local data before real use.
  • Epidemiological context carries much of the performance. With clinical findings alone the macro ROC-AUC was about 0.71; the patient's state of residence and month (local circulation) raise it above 0.80. That is legitimate pre-test probability, but part of the state effect may reflect how each state's laboratories test and close cases.
  • "Days since onset" is measured in SINAN up to the notification, not the consultation, so part of its weight may reflect reporting habits rather than the disease.
  • "Other febrile illness" is the hardest class (lowest recall): it is a mix of many diseases with no typical pattern.

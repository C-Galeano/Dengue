What does it predict?

Among patients with fever reported as suspected dengue cases, it estimates the probability that the case is confirmed dengue (by laboratory testing or epidemiological link) and not a discarded case.

  • Inputs: age group, sex, and {{ NOMBRES_SINTOMAS|length }} symptoms that the patient can report.
  • Excluded: fever (present in 100% of cases), laboratory tests, and variables known after diagnosis (shock, organ damage, hospitalization…), to avoid data leakage.
  • Data: {{ "{:,}".format(info.datos.n_total).replace(",", ".") }} cases, {{ '%.0f' % (info.datos.prevalencia_dengue * 100) }} % confirmed dengue. Source: {{ info.datos.fuente }}.
  • Decision threshold: {{ (info.umbral * 100)|round|int }} %. It was selected to detect at least {{ (info.recall_objetivo * 100)|int }} % of dengue cases: in screening, missing a dengue case is more serious than a false alarm.

Limitations

  • Moderate predictive performance (ROC-AUC ≈ {{ '%.2f' % t.roc_auc }}). Dengue symptoms are similar to those of other febrile infections; therefore, the actual diagnosis requires laboratory testing. It is a screening tool, not a diagnostic tool.
  • Many false alarms: with the selected threshold, only {{ '%.0f' % (t.especificidad * 100) }} % of cases without dengue are correctly classified.
  • Changes over time: {% for v in info.validacion_temporal %}{{ v.escenario }}: ROC-AUC {{ '%.2f' % v.roc_auc }}{{ '; ' if not loop.last else '.' }}{% endfor %} It works with the 2023–2024 pattern, but not with 2025–2026 (post-epidemic period). It must be retrained periodically.
  • Data from a single municipality, and age is only available in ranges.

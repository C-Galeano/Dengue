The problem

Dengue, chikungunya and Zika are transmitted by the same mosquito (Aedes aegypti), co-circulate in Colombia and Brazil and start with almost the same picture: fever, headache, muscle and joint pain, rash. In the first consultation the clinician must decide what to suspect, which test to order and how to follow the patient. Getting it wrong matters: dengue can progress to severe dengue, and the NSAIDs used for chikungunya's joint pain are dangerous in dengue.

How the objective evolved

  1. Original proposal: forecast weekly dengue cases (kept as the baseline forecast on the home page).
  2. First iteration: a binary screening model, dengue: yes/no, with Colombian SIVIGILA data from Tuluá.
  3. Current iteration ("smart hospital"): after the first evaluation the scope was judged too narrow. The tool now performs a differential diagnosis among arboviruses and explains its suggestion to the clinician.
Business objective
Support the clinician at the first consultation of a febrile patient: which arbovirus to suspect, which test to order and which warning signs to watch, with a transparent explanation.
Data-mining goal
Multiclass classification: dengue / chikungunya / other febrile illness, from symptoms, basic clinical and laboratory findings, comorbidities and the epidemiological context (where and when), with per-patient SHAP explanations.

Situation assessment

DataPublic surveillance microdata: SINAN (Brazil, DATASUS) for training; SIVIGILA Tuluá (Colombia) for external validation
ToolsPython, pandas, scikit-learn, XGBoost, LightGBM, SHAP, Flask, SQLAlchemy, Render
ConstraintsNo public dataset with numeric lab values (platelet or white-cell counts); Zika's public form has no clinical fields
RisksOverlapping symptoms; a model trained in Brazil may not transfer to Colombia; class mix changes between years
EthicsDecision support only, with a visible notice; transparent explanations; patient data stays in the app database
